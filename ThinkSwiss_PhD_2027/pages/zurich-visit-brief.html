The lab

Institute of Medical Genetics, University of Zurich. Director: Prof. Anita Rauch. Both a research group and a clinical genetics service.

Rauch group website Team Institute

Main research directions

  1. Finding new disease genes for rare developmental disorders, intellectual disability and epilepsy. Their largest body of work, e.g. UNC13A (Nature Genetics 2025), EIF3A/EIF3B, SCN2A.
  2. Deciding whether a variant causes disease, including lab tests of variant function with collaborators, and the national SwissGenVar platform.
  3. Reproductive genetic testing: screening couples before pregnancy and clarifying variants found during pregnancy (Praeclare). Links to our work
  4. Clinical genetics service: diagnosis and counselling, including for infertility, family history and abnormal pregnancy findings.

Their reproductive genetic testing work

  • Carrier screening before pregnancy. Exome-based screening of thousands of recessive genes. In their 2022 study, 44% of couples who were blood relatives and 5% of other couples had an at-risk result.
  • The problem of uncertain variants. In 118 ciliopathy genes, 20% of healthy people carried a reportable variant, but 50% carried a variant of uncertain significance (2023). Uncertain variants make it hard to advise couples.
  • Clarifying variants found in pregnancy. Deciding whether a variant in a fetus is truly harmful, e.g. testing Noonan syndrome variants in placental (chorionic villus) cells (2026).
  • Testing methods. Genome versus exome sequencing; sequencing-based detection of alpha-thalassaemia variants.

Praeclare

Clarifying genetic variants for personalised prenatal and reproductive medicine

  • UZH clinical research priority programme, led by Prof. Rauch with Prof. Bachmann-Gagescu (ciliopathies), Dr. Burkhardt (prenatal medicine, University Hospital Zurich) and Prof. Müller (neuroscience).
  • The priority-funding period has ended; the research continues and still appears in 2025 papers.

SwissGenVar

National platform for clinical-grade interpretation of genetic variants

  • A protected database of variants from Swiss clinical genetics labs, linked to clinical data, with shared expert interpretation.
  • All five Swiss academic medical genetics institutions and the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics; funded by the Swiss Personalized Health Network. Open to partner institutions only at launch.

Where we could work together

1. From a confirmed variant to a PGT-M test

Couples flagged by their screening need a PGT-M test for that gene.

My role: connect their variant classification to Panel Studio, so a confirmed variant leads straight to an STR panel design.

2. Fast panels for new genes

Recessive and inherited genes that have no ready-made PGT-M test.

My role: design panels for genes they choose with Panel Studio, test a few markers in Zurich, and complete validation at NUS.

3. Which markers work in which population

A marker that is informative in Asian families may not be in European ones.

My role: compare STR markers in European and East Asian data, starting with PKD1/PKD2 and possibly our other panels. A joint methods paper.

4. A full pathway for at-risk families

Screening, variant confirmation, PGT-M, transfer and confirmation in pregnancy, with each centre's clinical partners and within local regulations.

My role: the link between the two groups, possibly through a PhD in Zurich.

What I would do there

Connect the two halves, using PKD1 and PKD2 as the test case.

  • Weeks 1–2: learn how they classify variants.
  • Weeks 3–5: check which of our STR markers work best in European patients, using public data.
  • Weeks 6–8: add a variant-checking step to Panel Studio, so a confirmed variant leads straight to a panel design.

This costs their lab nothing. If it goes well, it can lead to a joint paper and a longer collaboration, and possibly a PhD for me in Zurich.
