The lab

Institute of Medical Genetics, University of Zurich, led by Prof. Anita Rauch. They work on rare genetic disorders and reproductive genetic testing, and they run two projects that matter to us:

  • Praeclare: improving genetic testing for couples planning a pregnancy.
  • SwissGenVar: a national platform for deciding whether a gene variant causes disease.

Why it fits

ZURICHFind at-risk couples
ZURICHConfirm the variant causes disease
NUSDesign the STR panel
NUSTest the embryos

They do the first half of the PGT-M pathway, we do the second half. A couple can only have PGT-M once their variant is confirmed, so the two halves belong together.

What I would do there

Connect the two halves, using PKD1 and PKD2 as the test case.

  • Weeks 1–2: learn how they classify variants.
  • Weeks 3–5: check which of our STR markers work best in European patients, using public data.
  • Weeks 6–8: add a variant-checking step to Panel Studio, so a confirmed variant leads straight to a panel design.

This costs their lab nothing. If it goes well, it can lead to a joint paper and a longer collaboration, and possibly a PhD for me in Zurich.
